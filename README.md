### Historical Background:
Before the 1630s, SoHo was used as a tobacco plantation until it was part of a land grant to partially free slaves.  During the 1800s, the city began altering the land to make it rural. Building projects were followed with new infrastructure and commercial establishments. In the 1850s, SoHo became a red light district. Upper class and businesses moved uptown, with factories taking its place. For the next 100 years, SoHo became a manufacturing district until factories relocated to southern states in the 1950s. In the late 60s and early 70s, with cheap rent and abandoned lofts, artists moved in. In the late 70s, art world elites opened galleries, making the neighborhood a cultural center. By the 90s, SoHo became known as a wealthy neighborhood with high end retailers, and the shopping district we know today was formed. 
### Notable Institutions:
SoHo is known as a place where art and history blend together. Places such as The Drawing Center and SoHo Cast Iron Historic District, preserve the part of history which makes SoHo the place it is today. The Drawing Center, a museum inside a cast iron building is dedicated to the art of drawing with works from emerging voices to experts. SoHo Cast Iron Historic District, a landmark area with the largest collection of cast-iron architecture in the world. 
### Economic Characteristics: (Includes Noho)
Based on NYC planning, SoHo is an area with a significantly more wealthy area compared to the rest of the city/borough as a whole. Its household income distribution and educational attainment is much higher with rent burden being the lowest compared to the others. 
### Architecture: 
Cast iron had been used in bridge construction, building beams and other elements. This is due to its ability to span longer distances than masonry (brick, stone, etc..), allowing more natural light. It also allowed more open floor space for arranging equipment and storage space. Castings could be customized with elaborate designs and be replicated as many times as needed, producing faster than cutting and carving stone. They could be disassembled and reassembled, and lighter to ship and handle compared to masonry.
